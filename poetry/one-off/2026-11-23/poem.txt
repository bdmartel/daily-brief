The wind comes up the valley and it does not knock.
It hits the hill and hits the hedge and hits me,
throws the oak leaves back across the ground I raked,
drags the maple's shadow left and right and left
like something late, like something chasing something,
pulls the dust off every ridge the tines left standing
and runs it out across the road and gone.

I lean the rake against the tree.

The bed is flat.
The light lies long on it.
The seed goes in the cold and waits
the way the cold waits, all the way to April,
and nothing out here has to hurry but the air.
