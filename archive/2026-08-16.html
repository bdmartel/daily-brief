Your day

Good morning, Ben. You cleared a real stretch of work at Tori's yesterday, and it shows — you got the crowded perennials divided and replanted, the lilies split, and that branch that was blocking the east entrance walkway is finally gone. On top of that you deadheaded the tiger lilies, gave the annuals their haircut, propped up the zinnias and sunflowers, collected seed from the annuals that won't make it through winter, and kept the Repels-All going so the deer leave the hostas be. You even exposed more of the roadside stonework and drafted that bare-root and shrub list with Tori and Bob, plus got your two sixty-inch pieces cut. That's a lot of loose ends tied off in a single day.

So today's a good day to pick your next thread. If you're heading toward Wilton, the pack list would help you actually get out the door with what you need, and there's still kale waiting to go in the ground down there. The tomato work is sitting right in front of you too — setting the T-posts on the row that already has them, then deciding whether you want more posts for the second row. And the broccoli's getting eaten, so covering that bed is the kind of small thing that pays off if you slot it in.

Now, the honest part: that whole tool-and-materials pile — the saws, the planer, the sander, the level, the reemay, the hardware, the gate latch and hinges, the temp, the extra board — has been sitting on your list ten days now, and emptying the soil into the wheelbarrow has been there fifty-five. None of it's moving on its own. Today's the day to either load a few of those into the truck or cut them loose so they stop riding along every morning.

Beyond that, the bigger picture is steady where it's been. The garden still wants its beds filled and seeded, the gate hung, and the fence stones pulled for mowing; Tori's has the long game of invasives, the woodland pathways, and the fall compost-and-leaf plan; and off in the background sit the taxes, a few home fixes, and mom's shawl. No need to hold all of it at once — just pick what the day can honestly carry.

In touch

You had a nice long back-and-forth with Kelley about the kids — catching up on how old they are now, whether they're both as festival-obsessed as each other, and how much time they spend together, and it sounded like you landed on it being a healthy balance. You sent Tori some hours and an expense to log, and you sorted out an 8:30 plan with Kelly. You also wrote a heartfelt note to your mom, apologizing for being short with her and trying to explain that it's not the helping you mind but the feeling of being pulled away from your own work — you made clear how much her wellbeing matters to you. And you told someone you'd swing by to air out Mr Higgins.

At the mirror

Take a breath, Ben. You know that thing where you keep walking back into a house that isn't yours anymore — checking the locks on a door that closed years ago? That's what living in the past feels like, and here's what's easy to forget: a chapter can be read, learned from, even loved, without you having to move your whole life back inside it. And while you're at it, let go of that old word "slow" someone hung on you like it was true — because it never was. Your mind just takes the scenic route, and you've spent years learning to point it exactly where you want it to go. So look at yourself and say it: I was never slow — my mind just moves differently, and I've learned to aim it. And my past is a chapter, not a home. I don't live there anymore.
