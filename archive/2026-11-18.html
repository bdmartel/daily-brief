Good morning
The handle of the stove door has worn smooth where your palm meets it, iron gone the color of river stone, and you know its give before it gives— the little resistance, then the swing, then the ash to be raked back and the kindling laid crosswise. Cold mornings teach a man to be exact with small things, to tend the one latch that must catch, to let the rest rattle loose until he gets to them. Outside, the hills hold their gray the way a kettle holds its waiting. You've spent a long day chasing numbers down their holes; let this be the hour the work has weight you can lift. The light comes slow and honest over the near field. The day you built waits for your hands— rise and meet it.

Your day

Tuesday, the seventeenth of November. The middle of the week has its own steadiness — nothing to prove, no edge to it, just the ordinary work waiting where you left it. The year's tipping toward its quiet end, and the tasks have mostly settled into the slower, longer kind.

Nothing's marked as pressing on the calendar today, so it's yours to shape. The gate latch, the measuring tape, the staple gun are all still sitting where they've been — no news there.

In the garden, the big pieces are still the gate work — measuring before you haul everything out to hang it — and the border stones that need pulling so it can finally be mowed, which means lifting the fence pieces to get at them. There's straw to grab from Robbins when you're out that way, cilantro to seed, and those slightly longer screws that keep coming up. Over at Tori's, it's the same wide spread of fall work: the bittersweet along the embankment and upper field, the buckthorn and pachysandra, and the late-season things like feeding the beds with compost under the shredded-leaf mat, potting up the bare-roots to overwinter, and taking insurance cuttings before a hard frost catches the tender annuals.

Around the house, the smaller stuff is still quietly there — watering the plants upstairs, the mirror and frames in your room, the AC unit to list, the garage to sort. And in the background the winter planning is starting to stir: the nursery order that opens early December, the germination tests for January, the grow-light setup to build. Plenty of threads, all of them patient.

In touch

You spent a good part of the day looking after Sara June, checking in on how she was feeling, sorting out the receipt for those HiBar bars and sending her your Venmo so she could settle up, and making plans for a possible trip to Waterville at her aunt's this weekend. You also confirmed with Aisling that it'll just be you and your mom coming, with your mom pitching in an extra turkey and some pies. And you helped your mom get squared away on the Vrbo account—passing along the new password, flagging that her number's now attached so she should watch for anything that looks like a scam, and walking her through the updated cancellation policy and fee changes you'd made.

At the mirror

Notice something, Ben — you've always equated kindness with absorption, like being good to someone means soaking up whatever they're carrying until you're waterlogged with it. But that's not kindness, that's erosion, and it leaves you with nothing left to give by noon. The truth is you can stay warm and stay yourself at the same time; your presence doesn't require you to disappear into someone else's weather. A candle warms a room without the room consuming the flame — you get to be like that today. So stand here and let this settle into you before you walk out that door. Say it to yourself, plainly: I can be kind without depleting — warm and present without becoming a sponge for someone else's need.
