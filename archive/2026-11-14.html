Your day

Friday, the thirteenth of November. The number carries a little superstition for some people, but all it really means here is the week's winding down, and there's something steadying about that — the stretch of days behind you, a quieter couple ahead.

Nothing's marked as finished since yesterday, and that's fine; some days are just about keeping the threads in hand rather than cutting any of them. The frost question is the one that keeps surfacing this time of year — if there are tender annuals you'd want insurance cuttings from, that window closes quietly on its own, same with potting up the bare-roots to overwinter. And the beds are ready whenever you are to take that finished compost with the shredded-leaf mat laid over top, which is the kind of work that pays you back slowly, all winter long.

In the background, the Reemay, the wire, and the weedwhacker are all still sitting where you left them in your mind. The nursery order is worth keeping half an eye on, since that store opens early December and tends to sell out, and a rough draft of the bare-root list would make that moment easier when it comes.

Beyond that, the usual constellation is holding steady — the garden infrastructure with the gate and the border stones, Tori's long list of bittersweet and buckthorn and the middle-lawn decision, the house odds and ends, the repairs waiting on the right luthier and printer shop. None of it's going anywhere. It's the kind of Friday where you get to choose your own pace with it.

In touch

You were checking in with Trim about that plant you'd already been given two divisions of — the one that grows like a weed in Africa — wondering whether it had already been brought in since Trim's over in London. You sorted out a Spotify playlist for Sara June, which had apparently gone missing after you accidentally deleted it, but you got it recovered and let her know it was working again. With Bob you were sorting out the wet shredder you'd left out before the rain, explaining you'd tucked it back in the garage by the grow lights with the fan going and a heat mat underneath to dry it out, and you made a point of checking it wasn't disturbing his or Tori's sleep. And you passed Tori a quick update on your hours, correcting yourself to say it was four hours today, nineteen in total.

At the mirror

Here's something you've maybe never let yourself believe: your warmth doesn't have a hole in the bottom of it. You've been treating kindness like a bucket that empties every time you give — so you brace, you ration, you walk away from people drained and a little resentful without knowing why. But kindness isn't you handing over pieces of yourself; it's you staying rooted while someone stands near your fire. You can care about what they're carrying without picking it up and carrying it for them — that's not coldness, that's the exact thing that lets you keep showing up tomorrow. So look at yourself and let this one land, because it's the difference between being generous and being gone. Say it to yourself now: I can be kind without depleting — warm and present without becoming a sponge for someone else's need.
