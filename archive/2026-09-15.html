Good morning
The plane of the workbench takes your palm before your eyes are all the way awake — that grain worn smooth as a creek stone where your hand has learned it a thousand times, the little give of the vise, the cold iron jaw that holds whatever you set in it and asks nothing back. You stand a minute with your thumb along the edge, the way a man stands who has counted a long ledger down to the last honest line and closed the book. Outside the frost is lifting off the low field, and the door swings open on its worn brass latch — your hand knows the weight of it, the pull, the swing. A long stretch of numbers is behind you, all of it faced honest and squared away — and the day out there doesn't ask for any of that, just for you to stand in it. So — up!
Your day

Monday, and there's a particular quality to a September Monday — the weekend's loosened its grip but the week hasn't quite tightened its own yet. That in-between hour where you can actually hear yourself think before the day picks up momentum.

Nothing's marked itself off since yesterday, and that's fine — some days are just about being in motion rather than finishing. When you're moving through the garden today, a few things sit near each other worth holding in mind: the garlic bed wants tilling and a buckwheat sowing, the potatoes could use more soil hilled up, and there's a seeding rhythm you've got going with the cilantro and the fall greens if the mood's there for putting seed in ground. The gate infrastructure is still waiting on that measure-first step before anything gets hung. And whenever you're near the workbench, the tape measure, staple gun, and the horses are all floating around unfinished.

Over at Tori's, the bigger shape of things is the middle lawn and the entrance redesign, both slow-burn projects that reward patience, plus the ongoing push against bittersweet and buckthorn along the edges. The seasonal clock is quietly ticking on a few fall moves too — potting up the bare-roots to overwinter, taking insurance cuttings before frost, and that whole cold-weather run of germ tests and grow lights that'll fill the darker months.

Closer to home, there's the smaller stuff — plants upstairs needing water, the frames and curtain experiment in your room, the AC listing — and the repair odds and ends waiting on a luthier and a printer shop. It's a wide field of things, but today only has to be today. See what the light and your energy agree on.

At the mirror

Notice something, Ben — that jolt of fear you felt this morning, that flare of frustration you tried to swallow? Those aren't malfunctions. Fear is just a finger pointing at what you actually care about, and anger is the alarm that goes off when someone stepped over a line you didn't even know you'd drawn. So instead of fighting those feelings, get curious about them, and then come back down into your body — into your hands, the roughness of real things, the dirt, the tools, the weight of what you can actually touch. That's where you steady yourself. Now say it: "My fear shows me what matters, my anger shows me my boundaries, and I ground through my hands in what's real." Say it again, and mean it.
