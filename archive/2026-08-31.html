Good morning
A jay works the corn all wrong-headed and sure, tearing a husk it can't finish, screaming its claim to a field that never once agreed to be owned — and you stand at the rail and let it. Not your fight. The dew is still on everything that isn't yet done: the gate half-hung, the list you carry like a stone smoothed by handling, the calls you owe folded small. Underneath all that, the big projects hum on as they do — Tori's land with its bittersweet and buckthorn and the long slow moss-garden vision, the taxes and that lingering subscription to cancel, the smaller home things like the mirror and the curtain and the air conditioner to list, plus the repairs and the seed sowings that have their own calendar. None of it's urgent this morning. Pick one real thing, do it well, and let the rest keep.

At the mirror

Notice something, Ben — you've never once thought a thought only a single time. The ones that stick, the ones that shape you, you rehearse them, sometimes hundreds of times a day without knowing it. So the real question isn't whether you'll repeat thoughts today; you will. The only question is whether you picked them, or whether they picked you. That's the quiet power you've been walking past — you get to be the one who chooses what gets said in the room of your own head. So plant your feet, look at yourself, and choose on purpose right now. Say it: "I become the thoughts I repeat — so I choose them on purpose."
