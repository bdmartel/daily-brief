Good morning
The dew hasn't burned off the low beds yet, and the rows you walked and named just yesterday are holding their shapes in the gray-gold light. Some things took. Some didn't — the empty bed tarped and waiting, patient as you learned to be. There's a mug going cold somewhere you set it down. You don't need to be ready — just come as you are, still soil-warm. What you planted is still finding its way up toward light. So — up.
Your day

Good morning, Ben. That poem's still hanging in the air, so let's just take a breath before the day gets going. No need to rush at anything — let's just see what's actually in front of you.

A handful of things have been sitting on the list a good long while now — better part of five weeks for most of them. The soil work is still there: emptying it into the wheelbarrow and sifting the grasses and nitrogen out. The screen at the bottom still wants restapling, and until that and the bracing get done, the bed prep can't really finish. There's the leaning thing that needs bracing back to steady, the general repairs, the reassemble-and-refill, and measuring and cutting that door frame. And a few Wilton and Maija errands too — cutting up potatoes to bring over, and hauling the dehydrator and water vessel for the nettle tea. And the sprinkler, coming up on a month now. I'm not going to dress these up as new, because they aren't — you know them by heart. So today's the honest fork in the road: pick one or two and actually knock them out, or let some of them go with a clear conscience. Either one beats carrying them another week.

Beyond that, the garden's the big living thing right now, and it keeps asking for attention — the tomatoes want staking and their T-posts set, the broccoli's getting eaten and could turn into a lettuce bed, and there are empty beds waiting on a decision about what goes in. Over at Tori's there's the endless compost turning, the shredding and spreading, and all that bittersweet and buckthorn that wants pulling before it takes hold. And the quieter background stuff hasn't moved — the tax and subscription cleanup on the computer, the seed orders and fall sowings, and the small repairs and shopping runs still waiting their turn.

Don't try to hold all of it at once, Ben. Just choose where the day starts, and let the rest wait its turn.

At the mirror

Here's something worth sitting with, Ben. For years you probably heard "slow" and let it settle into you like it was fact — but slow was never the truth of it. Your mind was always moving, just not in the straight line everyone expected, taking the scenic routes, catching things others sped right past. And the real shift, the thing you've actually earned, isn't that you sped up — it's that you learned where to point all that motion. That's not a weakness you overcame; it's a way of thinking you finally figured out how to steer. So look at yourself and say it plainly: I was never slow — my mind just moves differently, and I've learned to aim it.
